# Every reward is tied to Ready, not to activity

XP, levels, collectibles and progress bars are all driven by **Entries** reaching **Ready**
(a **Streak** meeting the current **Pass**'s threshold, including at least one answer on a
different calendar day), never by the number of answers given. Rewards are paid again on
each **Pass**, so repeating a known list is worth doing. Repeating a word within the same day earns zero XP. Multiple choice
is offered only for **Entries** at **Streak** 0 and never advances a **Streak**.

This looks unnecessarily stingy from the outside, and that is the point: a reward system
driven by attempts is farmable, and a child who farms it gets a high level, a full progress
bar, and a bad **School Test** result. **Ready** cannot be rushed, because it requires a
second day.

## Consequences

- Progress feels slow on day one. A whole list cannot be finished in a single evening, by design.
- **Effort** (words practised, time, days in a row) is displayed separately, so that working
  hard on a stubborn word is still visibly rewarded without inflating **Ready**.
- Cramming the night before a **School Test** is not supported by the reward system, even
  though it is exactly what a child will want to do. The countdown is the pressure valve:
  it shows what is not ready, early enough to matter.
- Completing a **Pass** resets every **Entry** below the new threshold, so a full bar becomes
  an empty one. This is the riskiest moment in the whole design and must be presented as a
  harder goal opening, never as progress lost.
